Asking pay is base salary only, before bonus and benefits. Titles with fewer than 5 answers aren't shown. About 39% of candidates who gave a location are in Georgia; the rest are spread across the U.S.

Why they move

Growth beats pay

Why they're looking, from 201 candidates who answered in their own words. Many gave more than one reason, so the bars add up to more than 100%.

46% are looking for growth or a next step. Only 2% named pay. 28% are on the market because of a layoff or a change at their company, so good people are available right now.

Where they'll work

Hybrid works for almost everyone

From 236 candidates. They could pick every setup they'd accept.

85% would work hybrid and 63% would work fully in the office. Only 8% will only work remote. You don't need a fully remote job to compete.

Systems

The software they know

Accounting and payroll systems named by 141 candidates. Many know more than one.

QuickBooks is the most common. If you run SAP, Oracle or NetSuite, ask about it in your screening questions. About 1 in 6 candidates knows each one.

What that means for a team of 10

At professional-services turnover, a 10-person accounting team sees about 5 or 6 departures a year. In finance and insurance it's about 2 or 3. Either way, most of those openings are predictable.

A year is 12 times the average monthly rate. It counts departures, so one seat can turn over more than once.

What to do

Five ways to hire better this year

  1. Price to the ask. Put the middle-half range for the role in your job post. Candidates' floor is close to their ask, so low offers lose people.
  2. Sell the path, not just the pay. 46% are looking for growth. Show the next title, who they'll learn from, and what they'll own.
  3. Offer hybrid. 85% will work hybrid. Only 8% insist on remote.
  4. Screen for your system. Ask about your ERP up front. Train the rest.
  5. Build the bench before you need it. Turnover is predictable. Keep a short list of ready people behind every seat so the next resignation is a phone call, not a crisis.
