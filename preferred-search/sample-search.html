Preferred Search

Sample search, start to finish

One Senior Accountant seat in metro Atlanta, from the first call to day one.

This is how a Preferred Search can move for a hiring manager. You write the scorecard. We invite people to apply. You meet only the top of a ranked list.

Example only. The hiring manager, the company, and every candidate below are illustrative. No real people or clients. Days and match scores show one way a search can unfold. A live search moves with the role and with who responds.

  1. Day 1

    You leave with a scorecard

    The intake call is about the seat, not a generic job post. You and the recruiter write what “good” means before anyone is invited: the close this person must own, the accounting they must already know, and what would take someone off the list.

    In this example, the scorecard says:

    • Owns a full month-end close, not one corner of it.
    • Has closed the books in industry, on GAAP.
    • Will be on site in metro Atlanta on the hybrid days you set.

    And the screening questions you wrote on the call:

    • How many closes have you owned in the last year, and for how many entities?
    • Which accounting system did you close in?
    • Which weekdays can you be in a metro Atlanta office?

  2. Day 1

    Your job meets a warm pipeline

    The same day, the role is posted and matched against people already in conversation with us about accounting work in metro Atlanta. You are not waiting on a cold post to fill itself. Anyone who already fits the scorecard is in the first pass.

  3. All month

    The right people get your questions

    Up to 300 candidates are invited to apply each month. Each invitation carries the screening questions from your scorecard, so an application comes back with answers, not a bare résumé.

    Every candidate is invited to record a video intro. When someone records one, you can watch it before you decide who to meet. A video intro is an invitation. Some people on the list will not have one, and the search does not wait on it.

    Invitations keep going after the first shortlist. That is how the bench for the next seat stays warm.

  4. Days 2–5

    You see answers before any meeting

    Screening answers are reviewed against the scorecard. A recruiter runs a phone screen with the people who fit. You are not on those first calls. By the time a name reaches you, the written answers and the phone screen are already done.

  5. Day 6

    Five ranked people, ready for you

    In this example the Fab Five is in your hands on day 6. Five people, ranked by fit to the scorecard you wrote on day 1. You spend your time on the top of the list. A video intro is included for a person who recorded one.

  6. Days 8–9

    You meet only the top few

    You meet Maya R., Jordan P., and Priya S. Chris L. and Elena K. are not on your calendar. They stay visible, on the bench, in case a finalist drops out or the next seat opens.

    Your meetings are about the work: a close they have owned, the system they used, and how they would spend the hybrid days. The first-round sorting already happened.

  7. Day 11

    You make the offer

    In this example you offer the seat to Maya R. We help you put the offer together. Jordan P. and Priya S. stay warm until she accepts, so a no does not send you back to the start.

  8. Day 25

    They start the way the seat needs

    You choose how day one works. In this example Maya starts Temp-to-Hire, so you can see a close before she converts to Direct Hire. Direct Hire and Payrolling are the other ways to start.

    This example

    Temp-to-Hire

    She starts on our payroll. You see the work. When you are sure, she converts to Direct Hire.

    Direct Hire

    She joins your team from day one. Use this when you already know the seat and the person.

    Payrolling

    We run payroll for the person, whether you found them or we did.

    Before day one, Staff Financial runs a credit check and a criminal background check. Those checks are for people hired through Staff Financial.

  9. After the start

    The next seat is already warm

    Chris L., Elena K., and the people who were close but not on this Fab Five stay in touch. When the next accounting seat opens, you are not introducing yourself from zero. The scorecard, the answers, and the conversations are already there.
